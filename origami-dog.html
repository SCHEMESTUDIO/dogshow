Origami Dog: Step-by-Step Fold Guide (With Photos and a Free Printable)

A folded origami dog face is one of the fastest, most satisfying paper crafts you can make, one square sheet, eight folds, no glue, no scissors, and about 15 minutes from start to finished face.

Below is the full step-by-step diagram sequence, plus a printable square template and answers to the questions people ask most before they start folding.

What You'll Need

  • One square sheet of paper, standard origami paper (kami) or a square cut from printer paper
  • A flat, hard surface for creasing
  • A pen or marker for the face (optional, for the final step)

That's it. No scissors, no glue, no ruler.

Fold by fold

Step-by-Step: How to Fold an Origami Dog Face

Each step below corresponds to a fold in the diagram sequence. Work on a flat surface and crease firmly at each fold. A sharp crease now makes every later step easier.

Step 1: Start Colored Side Down

Place your square sheet colored side down, plain white (or back) side facing up. This orientation means the color will show correctly on the finished face.

Step 2: Crease the Diagonal

Fold the square in half diagonally, corner to corner, forming a triangle. Press the crease flat, then unfold back to the square. This crease is just a guide line for the next step. You'll use it again in Step 3.

Step 3: Refold the Diagonal

Refold along that same diagonal crease, but this time leave it folded. You now have a solid triangle with the folded edge at the top and the point at the bottom.

Step 4: Fold Down the Ears

Take the left and right corners of the triangle and fold them down so each one meets the bottom point. This creates two triangular flaps. These become the dog's ears.

Step 5: Angle the Ears

Instead of folding the ears flat and symmetrical, angle each one slightly outward and leave a small gap at the top where they meet. This small adjustment is what turns flat triangles into floppy, three-dimensional-looking ears. It's the detail most beginners skip, and skipping it is the main reason a first attempt looks stiff instead of "doggy."

Step 6: Fold the Crown

Fold the top point of the triangle downward to form the crown of the head, tucking the tip behind the two ears you just folded. This step sets the width of the face, so keep the fold centered.

Step 7: Fold the Chin

Fold the bottom point up slightly to form the chin. This small upward fold adds depth to the face instead of leaving it perfectly flat, which is what gives the finished model a slight 3D "muzzle."

Step 8: Add the Face

Flip the model over. Draw on eyes, a nose, and a mouth with a marker or pen, positioning them just below the ear gap and above the chin fold for the most proportional look.

Choosing the Right Paper

Paper choice affects how crisp your folds turn out, especially on Steps 4-6 where you're working with multiple layers.

Origami paper (kami): Standard kami sheets are colored on one side and pre-cut into squares, most commonly in two sizes: 6-inch (15 cm) and a larger 9.5-inch to 10-inch (24-25 cm) square. Either size works for the dog face fold, the larger size is easier to control on your first few attempts and is a better fit for kids' hands.

Regular printer paper: Copy or printer paper works fine once trimmed into a square. It won't hold a crease quite as sharply as dedicated kami, so press each fold firmly with your fingernail or a flat edge, especially on Steps 2 and 3.

Free Printable Square Template

If you don't have origami paper on hand, print a square template on regular printer paper and trim along the guide lines before starting Step 1. A 15 cm (6-inch) or 24 cm (9.5-inch) square both work, pick the larger size if this is your first fold.

Common Mistakes That Ruin the Shape

  • Skipping the angled-ear gap (Step 5): Folding the ears flat and touching, instead of angled with a small gap, is the single biggest reason a finished model looks flat instead of floppy.
  • Not creasing firmly on Step 2: A soft diagonal crease makes Step 3's refold sloppy and throws off the whole face's symmetry.
  • Off-center crown fold (Step 6): If the top point isn't folded down evenly, the face ends up lopsided once you flip it over in Step 8.
