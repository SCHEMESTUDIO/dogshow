What "Finalists" Means at the National Dog Show

At the National Dog Show, a "finalist" isn't just any dog that showed up. The event moves through three stages: Best of Breed, then Group judging, then Best in Show. Winners advance from their individual breed to one of seven groups — Sporting, Hound, Working, Terrier, Toy, Non-Sporting, and Herding — before the seven group champions return to the floor to vie for Best in Show.

So when people say "finalists," they almost always mean those seven group winners — the dogs that made it into the final Best in Show ring, out of a field that saw the National Dog Show's largest entry list since 2019, with 1994 dogs across 201 different breeds taking part in 2025. Being a finalist doesn't mean winning overall — it means being judged the best representative of your breed's standard, then the best in your group, out of nearly 2,000 competitors.

Best in Show Winner

Soleil the Belgian Sheepdog claimed the title of Best in Show at the 2025 National Dog Show, an annual Thanksgiving Day staple. This is the first time the breed was victorious in Philadelphia, and the result was deemed a surprise on the NBC broadcast. George the American Foxhound was runner-up as the reserve Best in Show.

Judge Charlie Olvis explained the decision plainly: "Today, that dog was on," said judge Charlie Olvis of Soleil. "Didn't put its foot down wrong. Didn't miss a beat. And it is in drop-dead gorgeous condition." The competition wasn't lopsided, either — the final field for Best in Show was loaded, since Soleil and the other six dogs had all ranked No. 1 nationally at one point.

How Dogs Become Finalists

The judging process is more straightforward than it looks on TV. Dogs are assessed according to how closely they resemble the judges' "mental image of the perfect dog as described in the breed's official standard," with judges considering the "overall appearance, temperament and structure" of each dog, "looking for characteristics that allow the dog to perform the function for which his or her breed was bred."

In practice, that means three rounds:

  1. Best of Breed — a dog competes only against others of its own breed.
  2. Group judging — breed winners are sorted into one of seven groups (Sporting, Hound, Working, Terrier, Toy, Non-Sporting, Herding), and one dog per group advances.
  3. Best in Show — the seven group winners face off, and one dog takes the overall title.

Only purebred dogs registered with the American Kennel Club, which sanctions the event, can compete. The show has deep roots, too: it's founded in 1879, featuring only purebred dogs registered with the American Kennel Club competing across seven groups. On television, the 2025 National Dog Show aired on NBC for the 24th year and streamed on Peacock, hosted by Carillo, "Seinfeld" star John O'Hurley and American Kennel Club-licensed judge David Frei.

FAQ

Common questions

Who won the National Dog Show this year?

Soleil, a Belgian Sheepdog handled by Daniel Martin, won Best in Show at the 2025 National Dog Show — the breed's first win at the event.

What's the difference between a finalist and Best in Show?

A "finalist" is one of the seven group winners who advance to the final round. "Best in Show" is the single dog chosen from those seven finalists as the overall winner. Every Best in Show winner is a finalist, but not every finalist wins Best in Show — six of the seven go home with a group title and nothing more.

When does the National Dog Show air?

It airs on NBC (and streams on Peacock) on Thanksgiving Day, right after the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, even though the show itself is held at the Greater Philadelphia Expo Center a few weeks earlier.

How many groups are there?

Seven: Sporting, Hound, Working, Terrier, Toy, Non-Sporting, and Herding. Each produces one group winner, and those seven face off for Best in Show.
