What "Finalist" Means at the National Dog Show

A "finalist" isn't just any dog that showed up. Best in Breed is determined first, followed by First in Group, and the winner of each group then competes against each other for the coveted title of Best in Show. There are seven groups: herding, hound, non-sporting, sporting, terrier, toy and working.

So when people say "finalists," they mean those seven group winners — the dogs that made it into the final Best in Show ring. This year saw the National Dog Show's largest entry list since 2019, with 1994 dogs across 201 different breeds taking part. Being a finalist doesn't mean winning overall — it means being judged the best representative of your breed's standard, then the best in your group, out of nearly 2,000 competitors.

Best in Show Winner: Soleil the Belgian Sheepdog

Soleil the Belgian Sheepdog claimed the title of Best in Show at the 2025 National Dog Show, and the result was deemed a surprise on the NBC broadcast. George the American Foxhound was runner-up as the reserve Best in Show. The win went to a 5½-year-old female Belgian Sheepdog handled by Daniel Martin of Princeton, North Carolina. Martin and Soleil took home the $20,000 grand prize, beating more than 2,000 dogs in the competition.

Judge Charlie Olvis explained the decision plainly: "Today, that dog was on," said judge Charlie Olvis of Soleil. "Didn't put its foot down wrong. Didn't miss a beat. And it is in drop-dead gorgeous condition."

The competition wasn't lopsided, either — the final field for Best in Show was loaded, since Soleil and the other six dogs had all ranked No. 1 nationally at one point. As co-host David Frei put it, "We always have nice lineups, but it doesn't always end up that they're the No. 1 dog of their breed. Because sometimes not all of the Nos. 1 in their breeds are entered at our show. And for that to happen to us and have them all in the final, it goes unsaid that it makes for a nice lineup."

How Dogs Become Finalists

The judging process is more straightforward than it looks on TV. Dogs are assessed according to how closely they resemble the judges' "mental image of the perfect dog as described in the breed's official standard." Judges consider the "overall appearance, temperament and structure" of each dog, "looking for characteristics that allow the dog to perform the function for which his or her breed was bred."

In practice, that means three rounds:

  1. Best of Breed — a dog competes only against others of its own breed.
  2. Group judging — breed winners are sorted into one of seven groups (Sporting, Hound, Working, Terrier, Toy, Non-Sporting, Herding), and one dog per group advances.
  3. Best in Show — the seven group winners face off, and one dog takes the overall title.

Only purebred dogs registered with the American Kennel Club, which sanctions the event, can compete. The show has deep roots: it was founded in 1879 and has been held annually since 1933.

On television, the 2025 National Dog Show aired on NBC for the 24th year and streamed on Peacock, hosted by Carillo, "Seinfeld" star John O'Hurley and American Kennel Club-licensed judge David Frei.

FAQ

Common questions

Who won the National Dog Show this year?
Soleil, a Belgian sheepdog handled by Daniel Martin, claimed Best in Show at the 2025 National Dog Show, winning the $20,000 grand prize after beating more than 2,000 dogs. It was the breed's first win at the event.
What's the difference between a finalist and Best in Show?
A "finalist" is one of the seven group winners who advance to the final round. "Best in Show" is the single dog chosen from those seven finalists as the overall winner. Every Best in Show winner is a finalist, but not every finalist wins Best in Show — six of the seven go home with a group title and nothing more.
When does the National Dog Show air?
The two-hour show is broadcast nationwide on NBC directly after the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade from 12:00 p.m. to 2:00 p.m. in all time zones, and it streams on Peacock. The competition itself is held a few weeks earlier at the Greater Philadelphia Expo Center.
How many groups are there?
There are seven groups: herding, hound, non-sporting, sporting, terrier, toy and working. Each produces one group winner, and those seven face off for Best in Show.
