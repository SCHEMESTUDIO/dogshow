What "Finalist" Means at the National Dog Show

A "finalist" isn't just any dog that showed up. Best in Breed is determined first, followed by First in Group, and the winner of each group then competes against each other for the coveted title of Best in Show. The seven groups include sporting, hound, working, terrier, toy, non-sporting and herding. So when people say "finalists," they mean those seven group winners — the dogs that made it into the final Best in Show ring.

Being a finalist doesn't mean winning overall — it means being judged the best representative of your breed's standard, then the best in your group, out of a field that's grown to nearly 2,000 competitors most years.

2025 Best in Show Winner: Soleil the Belgian Sheepdog

A 5½-year-old female Belgian Sheepdog named Soleil won Best in Show at the 24th annual National Dog Show, handled by Daniel Martin of Princeton, North Carolina. Soleil outperformed nearly 2,000 dogs to win, capturing her seventh Best in Show and setting a record for her breed. She became the 15th consecutive Best in Show winner of the Thanksgiving Day televised show.

Martin and Soleil won the $20,000 grand prize, beating more than 2,000 dogs in the competition. The result was treated as a mild upset on the broadcast, given the strength of the field — every dog who advanced to the Round of Seven was ranked No. 1 in their breed nationally, a lineup co-host David Frei called exceptional even by the event's own high standards.

Judge Charlie Olvis explained the call plainly on air: "Not that the other dogs weren't on, but she, in particular, was on."

George the American Foxhound had unfinished business: he returned to the National Dog Show and this time earned reserve Best in Show behind Soleil, capping a career that includes an 11th Best in Show win.
