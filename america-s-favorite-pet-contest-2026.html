Every January, a new crop of cats and dogs starts flooding Facebook timelines with links to vote for them, and every year someone asks the same question in the comments: is this actually real, or is my cousin about to get scammed out of ten bucks? Here's what's actually going on with America's Favorite Pet 2026, how the entry and voting process works, and what to check before you type in a credit card number.

What Is America's Favorite Pet 2026?

America's Favorite Pet is a national online popularity contest for cats and dogs, run by the fundraising company Colossal in partnership with the nonprofit PAWS (Progressive Animal Welfare Society). Pet parents from all over the nation enter their loving companions for a chance to win $10,000 and to appear on the cover of Modern Dog or Modern Cat magazines. The competition has been running since 2020, and since 2020, America's Favorite Pet has been the biggest pet popularity contest in the country, pitting hundreds of thousands of cats and dogs against each other in a battle for votes.

The structure is straightforward: owners submit a photo and a short bio, the public votes in rounds, and the field narrows until one dog and one cat are crowned. America's Favorite Pet partners with PAWS to aid in its mission to help cats, dogs, and wild animals thrive. If you want the exact fine print — eligibility clauses, tiebreaker rules, disqualification terms — that lives on the official rules page at americasfavpet.com, not here. What follows is the practical version.

How to Enter Your Pet

Entry is free and happens directly on americasfavpet.com. A few things worth knowing before you fill out the form:

  • One entry per owner. Only one entry per person/pet owner will be accepted; all subsequent entries will be disregarded, and any secondary entries removed, with a limit of one entrant per entry submission. If you have three dogs, pick one.
  • You'll need a photo and a short bio. The site's actual entries read like short pet-parent letters — a few lines about personality, quirks, favorite activities. Nothing elaborate is required, but a photo that shows your pet's face clearly and a bio with actual personality tends to do better once voting starts.
  • Registration closes in mid-January. Entrants may submit their entry forms and photographs anytime on or before January 14th, 2026, the Entry Deadline. After that, entries stop.
  • Not everyone who enters becomes a "Competitor." The operator selects, in its sole discretion, which entrants will participate as Competitors, and upon entry, competitors are divided into groups and advance to the initial Top 20 public voting round. Getting in isn't automatic just because you submitted a form.

There's no fee to enter. Money only enters the picture once voting starts.

How Voting Works

This is the part most first-timers get wrong, and it's also where most of the skepticism online comes from.

Free voting exists, but it's limited. According to a legitimacy breakdown of the contest, daily voting is free, and all voters earn one free vote every 24 hours. Beyond that, single votes cost $1.00 each, and you can ask family and friends to pay to vote too. Some rounds have offered bundled donation tiers instead of per-vote pricing — votes can be entered for free with one or two votes cast daily depending on the verification method chosen, or people can cast 10 to 250 votes through a monetary donation that benefits PAWS.

The bracket runs in rounds, and votes don't always carry over evenly. Per the official rules: public voting begins January 5, 2026, and the Top 20 round, running January 5th through January 15th, uses public voting to reduce each group to twenty competitors. That's followed by a Top 15 round, then further cuts. Votes are reset at various stages, and public voting will determine the top semifinalist from each group who advances to the Finals. The Finals themselves begin March 6th and end March 12th in most recent cycles. If your pet's tally resets to zero between rounds, that's by design, not a glitch.

Ties are broken by the operator, not by a runoff vote. If competitors receive an equal number of votes in any round, the operator determines who advances based on competitor profile views. Worth knowing before you assume more votes always equals advancement.

Winners get cash and a magazine cover — not a plaque. The grand prize is a $10,000 cash prize and a feature on the cover of Modern Cat or Modern Dog magazine. That's a real, verifiable prize structure specific to this contest — worth flagging because not every online pet contest pays out cash, and you shouldn't assume one does just because this one does.

Is It Legitimate? What to Watch For

Short answer: the contest is real, it's run by an actual fundraising company with a real nonprofit partner, and past winners have collected the prize. The skepticism in search results and Reddit threads isn't baseless, though — it's mostly about the pay-to-vote mechanic and where the money goes.

Here's the honest version. Donations raised from votes cast during the competition go directly to DTCare, which subsequently grants the donations minus competition fees (36.5%) and variable costs (including payment processing, operating and prize costs, not to exceed 13.5%), plus a nominal 1% retained by DTCare, to the designated grantee. That means a meaningful chunk of every dollar donated covers running the contest itself, not just the charity. That's disclosed, which is the important part — a legitimate contest tells you where the money goes, even when the answer is "less than half, after costs."

The pay-to-vote structure has also drawn real pushback. One pet owner interviewed about the contest put it bluntly: "It seems to be that America's Favorite Pet can decide what the rules are and what they're not. This isn't an election. This isn't financial law. This is a completely arbitrary set of rules about a cat contest." That's a fair characterization of any popularity contest with adjustable rules and operator discretion — it's not evidence of fraud, but it does mean you should enter with realistic expectations about fairness.

Red flags to actually watch for, separate from the official contest itself: third-party pages or DMs claiming to guarantee a win in exchange for payment, links that aren't on americasfavpet.com or paws.org asking for "verification" payments, and any message implying your pet will be disqualified unless you pay immediately. The real contest doesn't operate that way — all dates in the official rules are explicitly noted as approximate, which is the opposite of manufactured urgency.

Other Pet Contests Worth Knowing About

America's Favorite Pet isn't the only game in town, and it's worth understanding how the structures differ before you decide where to put your dog's photo.

Some contests run on straight popularity voting with cash prizes and pay-to-vote mechanics, like the one above. Others run on a free, always-on format where dogs get stage time in a shared stream and earn honors through free or optional paid "votes," with no cash prizes involved at all — that's the model behind our own show, and it's a genuinely different kind of contest, not a replacement for this one. If you want to see how that mechanic works before entering anywhere, our breed pages and running list of animal competitions are a reasonable place to compare formats side by side.

The honest takeaway either way: read the rules page of whatever contest you enter, know exactly what a paid vote buys, and treat "guaranteed win" claims from anyone other than the contest operator as a scam, full stop.
