What Is Pupvote?

Pupvote is an international pet photo contest platform. It describes itself as a world wide pet photo contest where people from around the world can upload and compete by creating a profile for their loved pet. Dogs and cats are both eligible, and entries come from owners across multiple countries, with region-specific contest pages for places like the US, Canada, and Australia.

The photo contest mechanic

The structure is straightforward: create a profile for your pet, submit a photo, and let the voting begin. The pitch is simple — sign up for Pupvote and compete with dogs and cats from all over the world, with prizes advertised as $2,000 worth every week. Some contest pages frame the prize pool as monthly rather than weekly, so the exact cadence can vary by region and by which contest you've entered — worth double-checking on the specific entry page before you assume a payout schedule.

Who runs it, and is it legitimate

Pupvote isn't a fly-by-night operation. It carries a good Trustpilot score of 4.8 based on more than 3,000 reviews, indicating a high level of trust from customers, and the site is linked to a verified entity on Meta's platforms, which helps reduce the risk of impersonation or fraud. Reviewers on Trustpilot consistently mention the same things: it's easy to use, the pet profiles are fun to browse, and the community feels warm rather than cutthroat. One recurring theme in reviews is that owners enter dogs into contests, vote on other dogs, and get rewarded for supporting other owners, with a paid option to buy extra votes.

How Pupvote Works

Entering your dog

Signing up itself is free. Pupvote is free to join and allows you to win up to $2,000 every month in prizes, according to the platform's own contest pages. That said, a free account doesn't necessarily mean every path to visibility is free — some reviewers have noted friction around paid entry tiers for specific contests. One Trustpilot reviewer put it plainly: "So far so good but I'm not paying 20 dollars to enter a contest. I'm looking for one for free first." If you're contest-hopping on a budget, read the entry terms for each specific contest rather than assuming the whole platform works the same way.

Voting and prizes

Once your pet has a profile, the vote count becomes the main currency. Some users describe earning extra votes through platform "achievements," including unlocking extra votes through an achievements page, with some votes earned by following the brand on social media. Cash-adjacent language shows up across this whole category of sites — competitors in the same space, for instance, pay winners cash prizes via PayPal after the contest ends. Whether Pupvote's payout mechanics work the same way isn't something we can independently verify from public review data, so if a guaranteed cash payout is the deciding factor for you, confirm the current terms directly on Pupvote before entering.

Timeline and frequency

Contests appear to run on rolling cycles rather than a single annual event, with region-specific pages (US, Canada, Australia) each running their own entry windows. If precise start and end dates matter to you, check the specific contest page you're entering — cycle length isn't something publicly standardized across every Pupvote contest.

Pupvote Pros and Cons

Why owners love it

The recurring praise in reviews isn't really about winning — it's about the browsing experience. Owners talk about the pleasure of sharing fur babies with others and seeing all the other fur babies around the world while speaking with other owners. Another reviewer summed up the appeal as a site that's simple to use, quick for uploading photos, and good at bringing dog lovers together in a positive and supportive way.

Common friction points

The pattern that shows up most in less glowing reviews is confusion around cost — specifically, whether a given contest is free or whether winning meaningfully requires buying votes. There's also the general nature of any vote-based photo contest: results depend partly on how many people you can rally to click "vote," which rewards owners with big social networks as much as it rewards a great photo.

Pupvote vs. Other Dog Photo Contests

Pupvote vs. Instagram photo contests

Instagram contests (hashtag campaigns, brand giveaways, local business promotions) tend to be one-off events with a fixed start and end date, judged by likes or by a brand's own criteria. Pupvote is a dedicated, ongoing platform built specifically around pet photos and voting, which gives it more permanence — a profile that exists past a single hashtag's lifespan — but also means you're one photo among a much larger, international pool competing on the same clock.

Pupvote vs. a 24/7 livestream format

Photo contests like Pupvote reward a single great image and a vote-gathering campaign. A livestream format works differently: instead of one static photo competing for clicks over days or weeks, dogs appear on screen for a short slot while people watch and react in real time. That's the model Dog Show runs on — a live shared stream where dogs appear one at a time and viewers respond as it happens, rather than voting on a gallery of photos at their own pace.

Should You Enter Pupvote or Try Something Else?

Best for photo-focused owners

If you enjoy the process of picking the perfect photo, writing a pet bio, and rallying friends and family to vote over several days, Pupvote's format rewards that kind of patient, campaign-style participation. It suits owners who like a slower build and don't mind the guesswork around entry costs for specific contests.

Best for real-time, low-commitment fun

If what you actually want is to watch dogs — lots of them, live, without registering for anything — a 24/7 stream fits differently. Dog Show doesn't ask for a vote-gathering campaign; you show up, watch whoever's on stage, and move on. Entry is free, awards are permanent titles rather than prize money, and there's no pressure to hit a submission deadline because there isn't one — the show runs continuously. For a fuller breakdown of how the mechanics and voting work, the resources page covers the FAQ in more detail.

Neither format is objectively better. Pupvote suits people who like the slow burn of a photo campaign; a livestream suits people who want the payoff of watching dogs, immediately, without homework.
