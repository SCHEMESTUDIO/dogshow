What Are Jack Onofrio Dog Shows?

Jack Onofrio Dog Shows is a dog show superintending company, the outfit clubs across the country hire to run entries, print catalogs, and manage the paperwork side of AKC conformation shows.

A Brief History

Jack Onofrio worked as an AKC licensed all-breed professional dog handler before he and his wife Dorothy co-founded Jack Onofrio Dog Shows in 1968, which grew from fourteen shows per year to its current scale. That origin story matters because it explains the company's shape today: a husband-and-wife operation that started small and expanded as more kennel clubs needed reliable superintending.

Who Runs Them & Where

Jack Onofrio Dog Shows, L.L.C. is headquartered in Oklahoma City, Oklahoma. The company organizes and manages conformation dog shows as an American Kennel Club licensed superintendent. You'll find the Onofrio name on premium lists for events as geographically scattered as the Houston World Series of Dog Shows, the Rose City Classic in Portland, the Hoosier Kennel Club's Indy Winter Classic, and Rio Grande Kennel Club in New Mexico, among many others. The Houston World Series of Dog Shows lists Jack Onofrio Dog Shows, LLC as its superintendent.

The All-Breed Format

These are conformation shows, meaning dogs are judged against their breed standard rather than against each other directly. The AKC runs multiple sanctioned show types, but the ones Onofrio superintends are typically all-breed shows where dozens of breeds compete in the same ring rotation across a weekend, grouped by the AKC's seven breed groups before working toward Best in Show.

How Onofrio Shows Work

In-Person Judging & Ring Protocol. Every Onofrio-superintended show follows standard AKC conformation judging. Dogs move through breed classes, winners advance to group judging, and group winners compete for Best in Show. None of this is unique to Onofrio, since the company's job is logistics and entry processing rather than the judging itself, which is handled by AKC-approved judges contracted by the host club.

Entry & Registration. Jack Onofrio Dog Shows provides online and mail-in entry processing, exhibitor code management, and fax handling to support exhibitors. It prepares and distributes premium lists and show materials for dog clubs that contract its services. If you enter online, expect a small processing fee on top of the entry cost itself. A $3.30 non-refundable processing fee is added to each entry for use of the online service.

As for the entry fee itself, it tracks standard AKC fee structure rather than anything Onofrio sets independently. Entry fees include a 50 cent AKC recording fee and a $3.00 AKC Event Service Fee, with first entry of each dog running roughly $28 to $30 at a typical club show, and puppy or veteran classes priced a bit lower. Junior Showmanship competition often runs around $10, and some shows waive it entirely for juniors. Exact numbers vary show to show and club to club, so always check the specific premium list before mailing a check.

What to Expect as an Exhibitor. Bring your dog's AKC registration, show your own paperwork at check-in, and expect a catalog that lists ring times by breed and class. If you're new to this, Onofrio's own orientation materials walk through the entry form line by line, since getting breed, class, and registration numbers wrong is the most common reason entries get bounced back.

Onofrio Shows vs. Other Dog Show Circuits

AKC Shows & Breed Specialties. Onofrio isn't the only superintendent working AKC shows. Other firms like Jack Bradshaw, MB-F/Infodog, and Jim Rau handle different regional circuits, and some clubs run their own entries without a superintendent at all. If you're comparing options across the country, our guide to Jim Rau Dog Shows covers how that superintendent's territory and process differs. What they share is the underlying structure: AKC rules, breed standards, licensed judges, and a points system toward championship titles.

Online Dog Shows (New Alternative). Traditional conformation shows require travel, entry fees, grooming, and months of ring preparation. Getting Started: Do You Need Onofrio Shows?

Who Enters & Why. People entering Onofrio-superintended shows are almost always working toward something specific: AKC championship points, Grand Champion titles, or just experience in the ring before a bigger specialty show. If your goal is a title recognized by the American Kennel Club, there's no substitute for entering an AKC-licensed show, and checking a dog's prior registration and title history through a tool like the AKC dog lookup is a reasonable first step before you commit to a circuit.

Costs & Commitment. Beyond the $28 to $30 entry fee per class, factor in travel, grooming (professional handlers aren't cheap if you hire one), and the time commitment of a multi-day event. None of that is unusual for AKC conformation generally, it's simply the standard cost of competing at this level, regardless of which superintendent is running the show.

Alternatives to Traditional Shows

If the Onofrio circuit sounds like more commitment than you're ready for, that's fine, plenty of dog owners just want a low-stakes way to show off a good dog. Breed-specific resources like our golden retriever guide are useful if you're weighing whether your dog's breed is a strong conformation candidate at all. And for something lighter, a free online show format lets you skip the entry fees and travel while still getting your dog some genuine attention. Onofrio and its AKC-sanctioned competitors remain the legitimate path for anyone chasing a title, the newer platforms are simply a different kind of fun that didn't exist a decade ago.
