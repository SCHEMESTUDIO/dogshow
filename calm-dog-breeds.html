Best Calm Dog Breeds for Apartments [2026]

Basset Hounds, Cavalier King Charles Spaniels, Greyhounds, French Bulldogs, and Shih Tzus are consistently ranked among the best calm dog breeds for apartment living, thanks to low exercise demands, minimal barking, and a knack for settling quickly indoors. Two frequently-cited "calm giants" — the Great Dane and Newfoundland — have famously mellow temperaments too, but their sheer size means they're a better fit for larger apartments than studios.

"Calm" isn't one trait, though. Before you pick a breed off a list, it helps to know what you're actually optimizing for.

What "Calm" Actually Means in a Dog

Calm, low-energy, and quiet get used interchangeably, but they describe three different things — and mixing them up is how apartment-dwellers end up with a dog that's a poor fit despite checking the "calm breed" box.

  • Temperament (calm): how easily a dog settles and de-escalates after excitement. A calm dog isn't necessarily low-energy — some calm breeds have real stamina, they just don't run on a short fuse.
  • Energy level: how much daily exercise and stimulation a dog physically needs. This is the number that determines whether your downstairs neighbors hear pacing at 6 a.m.
  • Vocalness: how much a dog barks at hallway noise, doorbells, or the neighbor's dog. This is arguably the single biggest factor in whether a breed works in shared walls.

A dog can be calm-tempered but still bark a lot (some hounds), or low-energy but anxious when left alone (several toy breeds). For apartment living specifically, you want a breed that scores well on all three — not just the one that gets it onto "calm" listicles.

Best Small Calm Breeds for Tight Spaces

If square footage is your main constraint, size matters as much as temperament. Among the calmest small breeds:

  • Cavalier King Charles Spaniel — adapts to any activity level, rarely barks, and is genuinely happy in a one-bedroom, with a calm indoor demeanor that fits apartment routines.
  • Shih Tzu — bred for centuries as palace companion dogs, quiet, low-energy, and small enough to get adequate exercise from indoor play alone.
  • French Bulldog — compact, low-energy, and content with short walks rather than long runs.

Best Large Calm Breeds (For Bigger Apartments)

Size doesn't automatically disqualify a breed from apartment life — a lazy Great Dane beats a hyperactive Jack Russell every time — but bigger dogs need room to physically turn around and stretch out, so they're realistic for larger one- or two-bedroom units rather than studios.

  • Great Dane — genuinely low-drive once past puppyhood, but needs floor space.
  • Newfoundland — gentle and patient, though the grooming commitment is real: expect a thick double coat that sheds year-round and blows its coat twice a year in autumn and spring.
  • Greyhound — the exception to the "big dog needs a yard" rule; despite its size, it's genuinely one of the lowest-maintenance apartment dogs on this list once past its short daily walk.

Calm ≠ No Exercise Required

Even the mellowest breeds on this list still need daily movement. Even the most calm dog breeds require daily walks, training sessions, mental exercises, and lots of love — calm, silent, and low energy are not synonymous terms. Skipping exercise on a "calm breed" because it seems unnecessary is one of the more common apartment-dog mistakes — under-stimulated dogs of any temperament are more likely to develop destructive habits or excess barking.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the calmest dog breed overall?▾
There's no single definitive answer, but Basset Hounds, Cavalier King Charles Spaniels, and Great Danes are among the breeds most consistently cited as calm across breed-club and veterinary temperament references.
What are the best calm dog breeds specifically for apartments?▾
Cavalier King Charles Spaniels, French Bulldogs, Greyhounds, Shih Tzus, and Basset Hounds top most apartment-specific lists because they combine low-to-moderate exercise needs with quiet, settle-easily temperaments. The best dog breeds for apartments are those with calm temperaments, low energy levels, and minimal barking tendencies that adapt well to smaller living spaces.
Are calm dogs good with children and other pets?▾
Generally, calm-tempered breeds tend to tolerate household chaos well, but every dog is an individual, and supervision around young children is recommended regardless of breed.
Can training make an energetic dog calmer?▾
Yes, to a meaningful degree — structured exercise, impulse-control training, and consistent routines all help — but training works with a breed's underlying drive, not against it entirely.
Do calm breeds still need daily walks?▾
Yes. Even low-energy, calm dogs need regular movement for physical and mental health; calm is not the same as no exercise required.
What's the calmest small dog versus the calmest large dog?▾
Among small breeds, the Cavalier King Charles Spaniel and Shih Tzu are frequently cited as especially calm companions. Among large breeds, the Great Dane and Newfoundland are commonly held up as gentle, settled giants — though both need more physical space than a typical apartment provides.
Which calm breeds handle being left alone best?▾
Greyhounds, Basset Hounds, and Shih Tzus handle being alone better than most breeds and can manage several hours without severe separation anxiety, while Cavaliers tend to crave more constant companionship. No dog should regularly be left alone for more than about eight hours.
